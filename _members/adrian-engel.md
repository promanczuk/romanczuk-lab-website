Adrian studied biophysics at Humboldt University of Berlin and the Autonomous University of Madrid and is currently pursuing his master's thesis project in our group.
In his bachelor's project, he investigated the role of heterogeneity in the self-organized collective movement of a simple multicellular organism (_Trichoplax adhaerens_).
For his master's thesis he will apply likelihood-free statistical methods, such as simulation-based inference (SBI), to infer model parameters from empirical observations of collective animal behavior and compare them with those obtained from simulations of collective behavior models.
Furthermore, he is interested in broader theoretical questions concerning collective behavior, including the emergence of collective behavior in complex biological systems, the optimal distance of collectively behaving groups from critical points, and the homeostatic and evolutionary mechanisms underlying the emergence of criticality.
